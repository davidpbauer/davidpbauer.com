The Water Beneath

Best read slowly, in silence.

A reflection born of weariness and hope. In an age that mistakes motion for meaning, this piece is my witness — to what endures beneath the noise, and to the quiet vocation of higher learning that refuses to vanish, even in drought.

Once, the life of the mind moved like a deep and quiet lake. Its surface caught the light of honest curiosity; its depths fed generations who trusted that reflection was its own reward.

We built our halls and our hopes along its edge, believing that if we tended the waters — in study, in conversation, in community — they would not fail us.

Now the shoreline has cracked. The air hums with urgency — expansion, efficiency, visibility — as if motion itself could replace meaning.

We are told to run faster, to draw attention, to produce endlessly. But the dust we raise blinds us, and the noise drowns the stillness where wisdom once gathered.

They call it innovation.
We who remember know it as the drought.

And yet — the water has not disappeared.
It has only gone underground.

Beneath the metrics and the spectacle, it moves quietly through those who have not forgotten how to listen.

It flows in the patience of educators who guide rather than perform, in students who still dare to ask why when the world demands how fast. It moves in communities that prize integrity over image, substance over the shimmering mirage.

To live for purpose in this season is to dig — slowly, faithfully — into what endures. It is to believe that depth still matters, that meaning grows in silence, that knowledge is not a product but a covenant.

I choose to remain.
To stand still while the storm passes overhead. To tend what can’t be quantified — conversation, conscience, care — until the rains return.

And when they do, may the water find us waiting — rooted, steadfast, human.

A reflection on endurance, vocation, and the quiet renewal of higher learning.

I have given my life to higher education because I believe it can still be a place where human beings are formed, not just credentials granted. This piece is not a policy argument but a confession: that even as the dust storm of opinion, funding models, and technology rages, I choose to stay near the water. If these words resonate with you, you may be one of the people quietly keeping the springs alive.
